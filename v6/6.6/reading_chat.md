### Reading CHAT transcripts

Transcription annotations describe speech; they are not additional spoken words.

- **Speakers:** `*CHI:` identifies the child's speech; `*MOT:` and `*FAT:` identify mother and father.
- **Replacement forms:** `word [: replacement]` supplies the transcriber's interpretation. Read `tere [: quiere]` as an approximation of Spanish *quiere* ("wants"), not as a person named Tere.
- **Incomplete words:** parentheses supply omitted sounds: `no(t)` represents "not."
- **Overlap:** `<words> [>]` and `<words> [<]` mark simultaneous speech. The words still belong to the speaker whose line contains them; overlap does not transfer them to another speaker.
- **Repetition and revision:** `[/]` marks repetition, `[//]` revision, and `[x 3]` three occurrences. Interpret the repair while still coding the indexed target.
- **Uncertainty:** `xxx` is unintelligible speech; `[?]` marks an uncertain transcription; `[*]` marks an error. These do not automatically make the negation uninterpretable.
- **Context and codes:** `[% …]` contains transcriber comments, potentially including actions. `[+ …]` contains corpus-specific annotations; do not guess their meanings or treat them as Bloom labels. Timestamps are metadata.
